# ISO 27001 Survey Questionnaires (Non-IT Audience)

Three ready-to-build questionnaires written in plain, everyday language — no jargon, no acronyms without explanation. Each question is written to look and behave exactly like a Google Forms item, and an appendix maps every question to the ISO 27001:2022 clause / Annex A control it measures.

| Form | Who answers it | Time to finish | Purpose |
|------|----------------|----------------|---------|
| **Form A** — Staff Security Awareness & Practice Survey | All non-IT employees (admin, HR, sales, finance, warehouse, customer service) | ~10 minutes | Measure how well day-to-day work follows the organisation's information-security rules |
| **Form B** — Customer Data Protection & Trust Survey | Customers / end users | ~6 minutes | Measure how safe and informed customers feel sharing their data with you |
| **Form C** — IT & Technical Staff Controls Questionnaire | IT team, sysadmins, developers, IT managers, managed-service providers | ~12 minutes | Measure the maturity of the technical controls your certification depends on |

> **ISO note:** ISO 27001 does not require a "survey" — it requires evidence that people are aware of their security responsibilities (**Clause 7.3 – Awareness**) and competent to do their jobs securely (**Clause 7.2 – Competence**). These forms are the easiest way to collect that evidence from non-technical people, and they also feed **Clause 9.1 – Monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation**.

---

## How to build these in Google Forms (5 minutes)

1. Go to [forms.new](https://forms.new) → click **Add section** (⫲) wherever you see **── Section N ──** below.
2. Add each question with the question type named in bold (e.g. `Multiple choice`, `Checkboxes`, `Linear scale 1–5`, `Short answer`, `Paragraph`).
3. Toggle **Required** on every question marked `✳ Required`.
4. In **Settings → Responses**: turn **Collect email addresses** OFF and turn **Limit to 1 response** OFF if you want honest, anonymous answers — say so in the description, as written below.
5. Optional: **Settings → Presentation → Shuffle question order** for the rating sections, and add a **Confirmation message**: *"Thank you! Your responses help us improve how we protect information."*

# Appendix 2 — How to score the results (plain-language scoring guide)

Score each Form A section as the **% of respondents choosing the safest option**:

| Section | "Safe" answers | ≥80% 🟢 | 50–79% 🟡 | <50% 🔴 |
|---------|----------------|---------|-----------|---------|
| Policies (Q7–Q9) | Found + read + follow | Healthy awareness | Communication gap | **Clause 7.3 gap** — re-issue policies, run briefings |
| Passwords (Q11–Q13) | Passphrase / MFA on / never share | Good | Some risky habits | **A.8.4 gap** — enable MFA, publish a password-manager guide |
| Handling data (Q15, Q18–Q20) | Check before sending, shred, no personal devices, know retention | Good | Intermittent practice | **Clause 8.1 gap** — relabel data, add send-time checks |
| Phishing (Q16–Q17) | Recognises + knows reporting route | Good | Awareness without action | **A.5.7 / A.5.24 gap** — add one-click reporting |
| Physical (Q21–Q23) | Locks screen, challenges visitors, collects prints | Good | Habit drift | **A.7.7 gap** — signage + refresher |
| Culture (Q26–Q27) | Reports instantly, no fear of blame | Good | Fear-based silence | **Clause 10.2 gap** — publish a no-blame reporting policy |
